🐎 What is Horsepower?

Let a horse do the work and find out!

1. The story 📜

👷 James Watt

About 250 years ago, James Watt made better steam engines. People used horses to pull and lift things. So he needed a simple way to say how strong his engine was.

🐎 Watching horses

He watched horses pulling ropes to lift loads from mines and wells. He measured how much work one horse could do in one second.

💡 Horsepower

He said: 1 horsepower (hp) = the work of one strong horse every second. "My engine = 10 horses!" was easy for everyone to understand.

1 horsepower is like lifting about 76 kg (one grown-up person) up by 1 metre in 1 second.

1 hp ≈ 746 watts

2. Make the horse work! 🪣

The horse pulls a rope. The rope goes over a wheel and lifts a bucket from a well. When the horse walks 1 metre, the bucket goes up 1 metre.

3. Same work, different time ⏱️

Work stays the same if the load and distance stay the same. But doing it faster needs more power!

Both horses lift 100 kg by 10 m (work = 9,800 J).

6. Remember 🧠

🔨 Work

Work is done when a force moves something. Work = Force × Distance. Unit: joule (J).

⚡ Power

Power tells how fast work is done. Power = Work ÷ Time. Unit: watt (W).

🐎 Horsepower

An old unit of power. 1 hp ≈ 746 W. Used for engines, motors and pumps.

💡 Faster = more power

Same work in half the time needs double the power.
